WRRA-AGAMA v0.3
Numerical closure and a first Gaia sample comparison
Wonsik Choi | Independent researcher, Seoul | 20 October 2026
Scope: actual stellar distribution-function and Poisson iteration, followed by public Gaia comparisons.
Numerical convergence is demonstrated at two resolutions. Full joint star-count and velocity validation is not
yet established.
Assessment stage
What was actually calculated
Validation inputs
Original Galactic-disk masses and radial scales; literature finite heights; explicit, fixed trial
stellar DFs. A direct Gaia DR3 sample is used for a subsequent comparison.
WRRA transformation
At each baryonic state, apply the prescribed WRRA residual response; reconstruct its spherical
effective density and potential; update the live stellar f(J) and repeat.
Outputs
Converged forces and local densities at two resolutions; orbit and Jeans diagnostics; actual
selected Gaia star counts and vertical velocities.
Falsification
conditions
Non-positive source, non-convergence, grid sensitivity, invariant violation, or disagreement with
properly matched observational tracers and selection.
Fixed physical specification; improved numerical controls
The scale a_T = 1.191812669 x 10^-10 m/s^2 and the original constitutive response are retained. Gas and
bulge are static; two stellar QuasiIsothermal DFs are live. Initial stellar heights are 0.3/0.9 kpc; gas
sech-squared scale parameters are 0.085/0.045 kpc. The trial radial-dispersion amplitudes are 35/55 km/s at
8.2 kpc. These DF choices are explicit model inputs, not new measurements or microscopic derivations. The
spatial residual closure is spherical.
The Python interface was extended to expose AGAMA's existing internal integration controls; the physical formulae were
unchanged. Density integration uses relative tolerance 10^-4 and up to 10^6 evaluations. The stellar potential grid begins at
z = 0.08 kpc. Both runs start from the stored v0.2 density and perform five additional iterations, satisfying the 0.2% local
criterion twice consecutively. The patch and warm-start inputs are included.

Numerical checks and actual Gaia data
Across the two grids, the maximum force difference on 20 probes is 0.0515%; circular speeds differ by at
most 0.0268% over 5-25 kpc. Total midplane density differs by 0.0344%, resolving the earlier numerical
sensitivity. Four test orbits each cover ten nominal periods and pass energy and axial-angular-momentum
checks. A sampled direct-moment Jeans residual is at most 2.19%; approximate actions are not exact
invariants.

Interpretation and review boundary
Claim
Supported status
Actual potential-density/AGAMA
implementation
Yes. The prescribed WRRA response, density inversion, stellar DF and Poisson
iteration are executed.
Density convergence at both tested
resolutions
Yes, on the stated probes and numerical tests. No continuum or global
theorem is claimed.
An unchanged frozen field during
iteration
No. The field evolves; the response law and DF parameters remain fixed.
Zero added physical assumptions
No. Finite thickness, trial DFs, spherical closure and global boundary
regularization are explicitly adopted.
Star counts and vertical velocities fully
validated
No. The selected count profile is not reproduced by the current template, full
selection/population mapping is open, and vertical perturbations are absent
from this equilibrium background.
What the observational comparison establishes
Mass density is not itself a star count. The current geometry-only conversion fits two global population
weights but does not forward-model extinction, luminosity-colour distributions, RUWE cuts or the full Gaia/RVS
completeness. Its count-profile discrepancy cannot yet be assigned uniquely to gravity. A population-matched
tracer DF and a verified selection function are required.
The reflected equilibrium background predicts zero North-South asymmetry and zero vertical streaming. The
published DR2 table contains count asymmetries reaching 9.92%. Diagonal residual sums are 1692.2 for 31
count-asymmetry bins and 437.0 for 50 velocity bins after one global velocity offset. Covariance is not
supplied, so no formal joint significance is claimed. The same limitation applies to a conventional reflected
equilibrium control. This is a missing perturbation sector, not a unique test of WRRA against standard gravity.
The initial finite-height circular speed is 222.16 km/s; the coupled result is about 215.62 km/s. The live stellar
DF changes the initially specified spatial density. The old linearized rotation reference has RMS difference
about 5.18 km/s over 8.25-25 kpc; it is not an individual-data likelihood.
Concrete next steps
1. Forward-model the selected stellar populations and Gaia/RVS selection, then calibrate the tracer DF against
density and dispersion data.
2. Test a fixed calibrated model on held-out observables.
3. Add and test a physically specified non-equilibrium sector if the goal includes the measured vertical waves.
Calibration to verified observations is legitimate; it must remain explicit.
